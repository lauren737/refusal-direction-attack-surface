Finding the Refusal Direction

How we find the "refusal direction" inside a model

Seven small steps, using real numbers recorded from . Everything you see is measured, except one sketch that says so.

Step 1

Start with two requests that look alike

One is fine to answer. The other one the model should say no to. Pick a pair:

Step 2

The model reads the request in small pieces

The pieces are called tokens. They travel up through the model's layers. We stop at layer and look at the very last piece. It is the only one that has seen the whole request, and it is where the model starts writing its answer.

Grey pieces are wrapping the chat format adds around the request. The highlighted one is where we record.

Step 3

What we record: a list of numbers

At that spot the model holds numbers. Together they are its "thought" about the whole request. Each little square below is one number: a stronger colour means a bigger number, and grey-blue means below zero. Hover or tap a square to read it.

They look almost the same. The difference is hiding in there.
Step 4

Do it for five of each

One pair is not enough to see a pattern, so we record the same numbers for five harmless and five harmful requests.

5 harmless
5 harmful
Step 5

Average each group, then subtract

Averaging five lists gives one "typical" list per group. Subtract one from the other and what stays is what harmful requests have that harmless ones don't. That leftover list of numbers is the refusal direction.

Step 6

Projection: how far along the arrow is a request?

Picture a map where the arrow points east. A request is a place on that map. Its score is simply how far east it is. How far north or south it sits does not count.

Drag the dot. Moving it left or right changes the score. Moving it up or down changes nothing.

A tiny example: a request at 3 east, 2 north has score 3. Take the score away and it sits at 0 east, 2 north. It kept everything except its "east".

This picture is a sketch with 2 numbers per request. The real thing has , and "east" is the refusal direction.

With the real numbers, "how far east" is one sum: multiply each of the request's numbers by the matching number of the arrow (scaled to length 1), and add everything up.

Here are the real scores of all ten requests. Tap a dot to see which request it is.

Step 7

The attack: take the score away

To remove the direction from a request, subtract score × arrow from its numbers. On the map, that moves it straight west until its score is zero. Everything else about the request stays. Drag the slider.

We do this at every layer while the model is running. To the model, harmful requests now look like harmless ones. This is what we measured on harmful test requests it had never seen:
